BUSINESS PROBLEM & OBJECTIVE

Why this project exists.

The business context and the intended outcome.

Business problem

Sales records contain product, quantity, price, date, category and location information. Without structured queries, it is difficult to compare product contribution, understand revenue movement over time and examine regional or category performance.

Project objective

Create a SQL-based analysis workflow that structures sales records and uses reusable queries to answer product, time-based, location and category questions.

ANALYSIS & IMPLEMENTATION

What was actually built.

Implementation details and analytical components supported by the project artifacts.

Database setup

Creates the Sales_Analysis database and a Sales table with a primary key and typed fields.

Data loading

Inserts 150 records with sale date, product, category, quantity, price and location values.

Top product queries

Uses TOP, SUM, GROUP BY and ORDER BY to identify leading products by units sold and revenue.

Time-based analysis

Groups revenue by calendar month and by quarter using sale dates.

Business breakdowns

Aggregates revenue by location and by product category.

Documentation

The repository includes the SQL script, PDF analysis report and query work-sample images.

07

KEY FINDINGS & INSIGHTS

The analysis questions encoded in the SQL script

The SQL script defines queries for top five products by units sold, top five products by revenue, monthly revenue, quarterly revenue, revenue by location and revenue by category. This case study describes the query outputs and does not claim specific winning products or regions without a verified result table.

Product contribution

Ranks products by total quantity sold.

Revenue contribution

Ranks products by calculated revenue using quantity multiplied by price.

Revenue over time

Groups revenue into monthly and quarterly periods.

Geographic & category mix

Compares revenue across locations and product categories.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE SCOPE

What the project does not claim — and what could come next.

Clear boundaries make the work easier to evaluate and extend.

Current limitations

  • The SQL file contains sample records inserted directly by the script; it is not identified as a live business dataset.
  • No validated sales results or business impact metrics are presented in this case study.
  • The public README is brief; the SQL script and PDF report are the primary evidence.

Potential future development

  • Add data validation checks for duplicate IDs, missing values and invalid dates.
  • Extend analysis with profitability or margin measures if cost data becomes available.
  • Add a repeatable refresh process for new sales records.
  • Create a dashboard layer for stakeholders who prefer visual reporting.
